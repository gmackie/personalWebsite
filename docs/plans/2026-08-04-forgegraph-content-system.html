Overview

This plan turns the existing partial feed/hydration work into a full public content system with clear boundaries. ForgeGraph becomes the machine-truth producer for public status, events, screenshots, and clips; gmac.io becomes the live operations wall; gmacko.com becomes the curated portfolio/build-update surface; Obsidian remains the idea farm; and Substack stays a human-authored editorial outlet outside the operational pipeline.

The implementation bias is narrow ownership. ForgeGraph does not become a CMS, Obsidian does not become a dashboard, and Substack never receives automatic publication. The system should produce proof artifacts and scaffolding, then require human judgment for anything that looks like narrative.

Goals & Non-goals

Goals

  • Make ForgeGraph the canonical producer for public status, media artifacts, and public event history.
  • Turn gmac.io into a live proof surface instead of a mostly static dashboard with thin hydration.
  • Turn gmacko.com into a curated portfolio of current ventures and short build updates tied to real shipped work.
  • Export idea seeds into Obsidian without flattening them into AI-written essays.
  • Keep Substack entirely human-controlled and editorial.

Non-goals

  • Do not make ForgeGraph a general-purpose blog CMS.
  • Do not auto-publish long-form writing to Substack.
  • Do not remove the local YAML/Jekyll fallbacks until the dynamic feeds are proven stable.
  • Do not make Obsidian the source of truth for deploys, health, or public app inventory.

Core Decisions

ForgeGraph owns facts.Status, screenshots, clips, deploy freshness, and public event records all originate here.
gmac.io stays factual.No essays, no long prose, just live proof, event flow, and operator-level context.
gmacko.com is curated.Only promoted items become portfolio updates, venture proofs, or build posts.
Obsidian farms ideas.Idea seeds, hooks, and reflections go into the vault, not raw status noise.
Substack is manual.AI may scaffold outlines or asset bundles, but not publish editorial by itself.

Milestones

  • Phase 1 — Producer foundation
    Define public events, artifacts, and human promotion state inside ForgeGraph.
  • Phase 2 — Capture and redaction pipeline
    Generate screenshots and short UI clips that are safe to publish and attach them to events.
  • Phase 3 — gmac.io live wall
    Render events, artifacts, freshness, and “needs attention” lanes from the live feed.
  • Phase 4 — gmacko.com portfolio updates
    Promote approved events into venture pages and short build-update drafts.
  • Phase 5 — Obsidian and OODA export
    Generate idea seeds and outline scaffolds without overwriting human notes.
  • Phase 6 — Governance and verification
    Lock in review points, redaction policy, and end-to-end evidence checks.

Risks & Mitigations

RiskSeverityMitigation
Feed stays technically live but editorially thin because capture artifacts are null or stale. High Phase 2 is mandatory before calling the system successful; add stale-state UI and artifact freshness counters.
Raw event noise overwhelms gmac.io and promotion queues. High Filter to public-reality changes only; dedupe with dedupeKey; manual approval for promotions.
Sensitive UI or internal routes leak via screenshots or clips. Critical Default deny for internal/demo routes, explicit capture allowlist, masking policy, and manual review for new apps.
gmacko.com becomes a second live dashboard instead of a portfolio. Medium Only render approved build updates and venture proof slices; no raw event stream on the homepage.
Obsidian fills with low-signal seeds that nobody revisits. Medium Only export events that trigger a real idea; add status: seed and dedupe exported notes.

Verification

  • Producer: schema/typecheck, feed route tests, and direct curl of manifest, apps, events, and artifacts.
  • Capture: at least one fresh screenshot and one short clip attached to a public event, with a visible stale-path when capture fails.
  • gmac.io: browser verification that recent events, proof rail, and freshness states render from live data and degrade gracefully.
  • gmacko.com: one approved event produces one build-update draft and one venture page proof section.
  • Obsidian/OODA: one approved event creates one seed note and one outline scaffold without overwriting existing vault notes.
  • Editorial guardrail: no automation path can publish directly to Substack or mark AI-generated draft copy as final.

Open Questions

Needs a decision: should promotion review live inside a ForgeGraph admin UI first, or should v1 ship as a CLI/operator flow that writes promotion_decisions rows and exports drafts/seeds from there? Recommended default: CLI/operator flow first, then UI after the object model is stable.
